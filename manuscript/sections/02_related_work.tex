\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Application and Mobile Manipulation}
Vega provides a relevant hazardous-environment precedent: Bird \emph{et al.} describe a small ground robot for nuclear decommissioning, with engineering directed toward its operating environment~\cite{bird2022vega}. That work motivates remote intervention but does not establish environmental qualification for general-purpose embedded components. Stretch combines a differential mobile base and compact manipulator, supported by design analysis and physical task demonstrations~\cite{kemp2022stretch}. HomeRobot treats navigation, recognition, grasping, and placement as a connected mobile-manipulation problem, with distinct simulation and real-world evaluation components~\cite{yenamandra2023homerobot}. These studies motivate HazBot's complete retrieval sequence rather than a target-following objective.

\subsection{Embedded Integration and Coordination}
ROS 2 provides a common communication and software framework for distributed robotic components~\cite{macenski2022ros2}. Nav2~\cite{macenski2020marathon2} and SLAM Toolbox~\cite{macenski2021slamtoolbox} provide established navigation and mapping capabilities. Their availability does not by itself demonstrate successful localization or navigation in a particular assembly. Likewise, edge-device detector evaluations show why model and device choices require explicit characterization~\cite{alqahtani2026edge}; their measurements cannot be transferred to HazBot's inference configuration.

Command arbitration and sensor-dependent stopping already exist in systems such as \code{twist\_mux} and Nav2 Collision Monitor~\cite{twistmux,nav2collision}. HazBot does not claim a new general arbitration or collision-avoidance algorithm. Its contribution is the explicit connection between a four-subsystem embedded design, retrieval-phase prerequisites, and reproducible characterization of the particular geometry and gateways that implement those interfaces. The reference coordinator makes this integration inspectable before a physical mission benchmark is attempted.
