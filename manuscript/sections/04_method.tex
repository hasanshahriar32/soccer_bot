\section{Retrieval Coordination and Analysis Method}
\subsection{Mission and Evidence Contracts}
The mission consists of SEARCH, APPROACH, DOCK, GRASP, CARRY, PLACE, and COMPLETE (Fig.~\ref{fig:mission}). SEARCH selects an authorized candidate. APPROACH moves the base toward its pickup region. DOCK transfers control from base motion to positioning the arm. GRASP secures the object, CARRY transports it with the arm stowed, and PLACE releases it at the designated deposit location. COMPLETE closes the task only after a release indication.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{hazbot_mission.pdf}
\caption{Seven-phase reference retrieval mission. Conditions summarize the handoff evidence; complete command predicates are defined in the text and executable model. A failed prerequisite requests HOLD at the current phase. The strip depicts the coordination specification, rather than a measured physical trajectory.}
\label{fig:mission}
\end{figure*}

Let $T$ denote an authorized, fresh target; $S$ a fresh, usable scan; $C$ a clear route; $A$ a stowed arm; and $B$ a stopped base. The principal command contracts are
\begin{align}
\text{base motion} &\Rightarrow S\land C\land A,\label{eq:baseguard}\\
\text{target approach} &\Rightarrow T,\\
\text{arm motion} &\Rightarrow B.\label{eq:armguard}
\end{align}
Reach and gripper closure additionally require $T$ and a reachable wrist target. Release requires the deposit region and a grip indication. The reference controller also requires scan health during docking, grasping, and placement. A failed prerequisite produces HOLD without phase advancement. HOLD means no active-motion request and must be implemented as an explicit stop at an actuator boundary. Logical transitions on target acquisition and release may also emit HOLD. A stop request and a stopped-base observation are distinct events: a deployed implementation needs measured feedback and a defined stop-confirmation rule to instantiate $B$.

The abstract observations comprise twelve Boolean values: target authorization and freshness; scan freshness and usability; route clearance; arm stowage; base stopped; wrist reachability; pickup-region arrival; grip confirmation; deposit-region arrival; and release confirmation. Each has a specific role in the interface, but this discrete model does not generate these observations from physical sensors. In particular, grip and release indications are input assumptions, not inferred force or contact measurements.

\subsection{Bounded Reference-Model Verification}
We enumerate every observation valuation at every phase: $7\times2^{12}=28{,}672$ phase--observation pairs per policy. No valuations are pruned. A separate admissibility oracle checks the output action against Eqs.~\ref{eq:baseguard}--\ref{eq:armguard} and the target, reach, and placement conditions. Breadth-first exploration establishes phase reachability and extracts shortest-prefix counterexamples. Three ablations remove the base--arm interlock, target authorization, or scan-age requirement from the controller while retaining the full oracle. This design tests whether each omitted condition creates a concrete contract violation.

The enumeration includes observation combinations that may be physically inconsistent. Consequently, counts describe the complete Boolean specification domain rather than probabilities or frequencies of robot failure. A nominal abstract trace checks that admissible observations permit progression to COMPLETE. Neither this trace nor phase reachability measures physical mission success.

\subsection{Source-Derived Wrist Geometry}
Figure~\ref{fig:workspace} connects arm geometry to the docking prerequisite. From the URDF chain, the shoulder lies at $(0.100,0,0.155)$\,m in the base frame and both positioning links have length $L_1=L_2=0.100$\,m. The zero pose has a vertical upper link and horizontal forearm. The figure uses $q_1=q_b$, $q_2=q_s$, and $q_3=q_e$. For yaw $q_b$, shoulder angle $q_s$, and elbow angle $q_e$, the wrist origin is
\begin{align}
r &=L_1\sin q_s+L_2\cos(q_s+q_e),\\
x &=0.100+r\cos q_b,\quad y=r\sin q_b,\\
z &=0.155+L_1\cos q_s-L_2\sin(q_s+q_e).
\label{eq:fk}
\end{align}
We sample 41 values, including both bounds, for each positioning joint. The declared bounds are $q_b\in[-1.5708,1.5708]$, $q_s\in[-0.5,2.6179]$, and $q_e\in[-1.5708,2.6179]$\,rad. An independent homogeneous-transform implementation evaluates the URDF chain at 1,000 seeded random poses and 13 special poses. Agreement checks numerical implementation, while the grid characterizes the ideal wrist envelope. The wrist origin is not a calibrated grasp contact point; collision, load, compliance, and stability constraints are not represented.

\subsection{Command-Interface Characterization}
We isolate the actual wheel gateway's \code{SET:} branch using Python's abstract syntax tree and replace only its transport sender with an in-memory recorder. All integer pairs $(l,r)\in[-255,255]^2$ are executed and compared with an independently specified sign partition. The arm gateway's pure angle mapper is evaluated for five accepted aliases over 451 inputs from $-90$ to $360$, checking clamps and reversals. The elbow has two aliases, so this represents four actuator channels. No ROS constructors, sockets, motor servers, or hardware launchers are executed. Source hashes, settings, CSV outputs, and validation records bind the results to the analyzed implementation.
