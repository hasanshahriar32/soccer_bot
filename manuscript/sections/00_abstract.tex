\begin{abstract}
Retrieving an object from a potentially hazardous area requires coordinated perception, mobility, and manipulation. We present HazBot, a modular mobile-manipulation architecture centered on a Raspberry Pi, with hazardous-object retrieval as its application objective. Four subsystems provide LiDAR navigation, differential wheel drive, camera-based onboard inference, and an articulated gripper. The camera connects directly to the Pi; dedicated Arduino controllers serve the wheels and arm, while the LiDAR retains its sensor-specific controller. We formalize a seven-phase retrieval mission and the evidence required at each subsystem handoff. A ball-based nonhazardous surrogate motivates the task; evaluation uses an executable discrete coordination model, source-isolated command analysis, and URDF-derived geometry. Exhaustive characterization covers 261,121 wheel-command pairs and reveals direction-only magnitude reduction and 1,020 mixed-zero inputs that emit no new command. The URDF link lengths yield a 0.200\,m shoulder-to-wrist bound; 68,921 joint samples characterize the modeled geometric envelope. The full reference coordinator admits no command-contract violations across 28,672 phase--observation pairs. These results connect the mission design to concrete interface and reachability constraints. The contribution is a reproducible architecture and coordination study that supports task-specific recognition and subsequent application validation.
\end{abstract}
